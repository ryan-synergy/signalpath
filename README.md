# SignalPath

**CAD-style AV system schematics in the browser.** SignalPath turns a room-by-room description of a residential AV job into an 11×17 engineering sheet — equipment rack, zone cards, signal wiring with hop arcs, title block — plus channel map, takeoff, wire schedule, and BOM comparison pages. Built for [Synergy Audio Video Systems](https://github.com/ryan-synergy) as part of the Synergy Field Kit.

**Live app:** https://ryan-synergy.github.io/signalpath/

No build step, no dependencies, no server. Pure static HTML + ES modules.

---

## What it does

- **Draw a full system schematic** from structured job data: sources, matrices, amps, and switches in a three-column rack; per-room zone cards with display/speaker glyphs; wires routed through a structured channel router with crossing hops, trunk-run bus ticks (`×N`), and a dynamic legend.
- **Five signal types**, color-coded (and dash-coded in grayscale/print mode): video, audio, audio return, network, pre-wire.
- **Multiple solutions per job** (e.g. "Savant AVB" vs "Dante" bids) with per-solution endpoint overrides, a solution-aware advisor, and a side-by-side BOM comparison page.
- **A verified device catalog** (55+ SKUs — Savant, Sonance, Anthem, AudioControl, AVPro Edge, Sonos) with typed I/O counts sourced from manufacturer datasheets. The advisor checks port budgets (analog ins, module outs, digital return ins), licensing tiers, and gear-specific gotchas (AVB switch requirements, Sonos lip-sync buffering).
- **Print pages:** channel map (per-device port tables), takeoff (NEW/OFE rollup + CSV export), wire schedule (numbered runs with cable totals), BOM compare. Long jobs paginate onto continuation sheets with true "Sheet N of M" numbering. One-click print to 17×11 landscape.
- **Quick-add / dictation entry:** type or speak `family room 5.1 75 sony matrix, patio landscape 8 prewire` and get parsed zones with preview chips. Add `avr` (or `receiver`) and the zone comes wired: an AV receiver in the rack feeds the TV (through an HDBaseT balun) and drives the speakers — the Anthem that fits the room (5.1 → MRX 540, 7.1 → MRX 740, 7.1.4 → MRX 1140; pick another on GEAR), fed from the matrix on a whole-home rack; `matrix` feeds the TV from the rack's HDMI matrix; `local` puts an Apple TV at the TV and wires it; a powered soundbar is fed by its TV over eARC; a `prewire` TV gets its pre-wire run back to the matrix. A room with nothing in it ("bonus room future") is flagged in the preview and skipped with a note, rather than silently dropped. `7.1.4` reads "7.1.4 Atmos" everywhere and has its own button in the zone editor.
- **The side panel, zone- and box-first:**
  - *ZONES*: each row reads closed — a ✓ / ! mark, what's in the zone and what feeds it ("5.1 surround + 75\" TV · TV ← Axion 8 · speakers ← MRX 540 8K · eARC"), and the advisor's warning for that zone right on the row. Open, the essentials are taps (speaker setup, TV/projector, size presets), then Hookup; name, scope, status, remote and the size check sit behind **More**.
  - *GEAR*: each box is its back panel — **Inputs** and **Outputs** listing what's plugged in (each editable in place), jack use against the catalog ("HDMI 2 of 7", "zones 6 of 8", matching the advisor), and ＋ buttons to plug something in or connect an output.
  - *JOB → All connections*: the full list with search, for checking — no longer the main way to wire.
  - Tapping a zone, box or wire on the drawing focuses just that item in the panel. The panel is resizable (drag its edge; remembered per browser).
- **Commands (type what you want):** the quick-add box also takes changes — "connect cable box to mrx", "kitchen, office and dining to amp 2", "family room from the receiver", "master bed tv direct hdmi", "master optical backup", "patio 75 ofe", "gym prewire", "rename kitchen to Chef's Kitchen", "add an mrx 1140", "delete the turn table" (several: separate with ";"). It previews exactly what will happen before Enter; **↶ Undo** (or ⌘Z) takes back the last command or AI change.
- **✦ AI — Claude drives it:** describe a change in plain words. Claude proposes it as commands; the app runs them on a copy, checks the result with the validator, and shows a preview (each step ✓/✗, problems before → after) that you apply or discard — nothing changes by itself. Three ways in:
  - *Copy prompt for Claude* → paste into claude.ai → paste the reply back. No setup, no key.
  - *Ask Claude* built in: ⚙ Settings → AI → your own API key (stored in this browser only), or a **proxy** that holds the key for the whole crew (`proxy/worker.js`, a Cloudflare Worker — deploy steps are in its header). The built-in AI gets the validator's verdict back and corrects itself (up to two passes) before you see the preview.
  - *For an outside agent* (Claude in Chrome, Claude Code): `window.SignalPath.summary() / vocabulary() / issues() / preview(cmds) / apply(cmds) / undo()`.
- **Rack starting points:** ＋ (new job) offers Savant whole-home (sets the job to Savant + AVB audio, so Savant host/licensing advice follows; the music server rides the AVB network — it's digital-only, and outside a Savant audio system it goes optical into the amp), Theater receiver, AV-over-IP (MXNet) or a blank rack — gear catalog-linked, the patching inside the rack done — and the quick-add box suggests the zones line for it. Quick-add then wires each zone: `matrix` feeds the TV, stereo-type speakers take the amp's next free outputs, surround gets a receiver fed from the matrix, `avr` gives the zone its own receiver.
- **Zone hookup:** every zone row has a HOOKUP section — *TV video* from (receiver, matrix, AV-over-IP switch, splitter, source, or gear in the zone), *Run to TV* (HDBaseT balun / MXNet decoder / direct HDMI), *Speakers* from (receiver or amp — an amp gets its next free outputs automatically), *TV audio back* — **eARC over the HDMI** (the default whenever a receiver feeds the TV; no extra run), **eARC + optical backup** (adds a Toslink run to the receiver), **optical only** (to a receiver or audio input module), or none. eARC shows on the channel map and on the video run in the wire schedule; only optical adds a pulled run. When eARC comes back through an HDBaseT balun or MXNet decoder, the advisor asks you to confirm that model passes eARC (many only pass ARC) until the optical backup is added. "＋ New AV receiver…" / "＋ New multi-zone amp…" add the box in place, and when a receiver already does half the zone a one-click button offers the other half. It all writes ordinary connections, visible in GEAR → Connections.
- **Importers** for SiteWalk survey JSON and Blueprinted (Savant config) exports, with non-destructive re-import merge.
- **Zone grouping (JOB → Group zones).** Default **by type** — the Synergy sheet: the **top band** starts with surround rooms with TVs, then TVs with 2-channel speakers or a soundbar, left to right, wrapping downward as the page fills; **TV-only rooms** sit in the **mid band** right of the rack, between the TV rows and the speaker-only rooms; **speaker-only rooms** fill the bottom-right, bottom-aligned with the rack (added order kept inside each group). Zones sit tight: 20px between cards, 24px between speaker rows; a wide card (a landscape array) spans whole grid cells instead of spreading every column apart. **By type, outdoor zones separate** draws outdoor rooms as their own OUTDOOR cluster (name-based — patio, pool, yard… — or landscape speakers; each zone's More → Outdoor overrides the guess). **In the order I added them** keeps the old behaviour. The same outdoor test puts rooms under Exterior in the PlanQueue export.
- **Markdown exports.** **PlanQueue** saves the proposal-import file exactly to Synergy's PlanQ format sheet (2026-09-28): a front matter block holding only `planqueue: proposal-import/1`, a `# Client — Address` title, `## Room` headers, and `- qty | Manufacturer | Model` lines — no notes, floors, tables or prose, and no mandatory lines (EWR, PM fee, materials are added in PlanQueue). Rack gear and TV-side adapters go by their **PlanQueue SKU** (catalog `partNo`, synced to Synergy's PlanQueue product export of 2026-09-28 — SKUs only, never its dealer prices; gear PlanQueue doesn't carry goes by its manufacturer order code and imports as a needs-SKU line). What notes used to say is said with PlanQueue's own items: owner-supplied and provider gear → **Customer-Supplied** TV / Speakers / Soundbar / Subwoofer / Equipment; speakers without a model → **Synergy · Unspecified Speakers** with the speaker count as the quantity (the sub on its own line); a TV without a model → **Synergy · Unspecified TV**; pre-wire rooms → Synergy's **Prewire - …** items (TV (Standard), 5.1 / 7.1 / 7.2.4 Atmos, Speaker Pair / Single, Landscape Speakers / Subwoofer, Passive Soundbar (LCR) when an amp drives the bar). Outdoor rooms come last; a room name repeated on two floors carries its floor. A job with pre-wire-only rooms saves two files (systems + "— Pre-Wire"); future rooms stay out. **AI .md** saves a review file for an agent: rooms and how each is fed, rack usage, open items, advisor notes, licensing, the switch port plan + outlet budget, connections, wire list, the quote lines, the command vocabulary, and the full job as a JSON block — which imports straight back into SignalPath (import takes the last ```json fence).
- **Network & Power sheet** (JOB → Packet pages; on by default). Every switch gets a port plan — which box lands on which port, on which network: MXNet ports (DANTE-DV2/EV2 marked "MXNet + VLAN 99", with the VLAN 99 trunk to the Dante switch), the Dante switch (controller, Director/Hyperion amps, AXIS encoders, Dante bridges), AVB, and the **house LAN**: every TV, every networked rack box (sources, receivers, amps, matrix, control hosts, the WattBox itself, a CBOX's control port) and one uplink per AV network. PoE is shown per port against the switch's budget. Rack gear numbers from port 1, TVs by zone, uplinks on the SFP cages. Under it, the **rack outlet budget**: one outlet per rack box (catalog `outlets` for dual-cord gear) except PoE gear its switch powers, plus one per rack-fed HDBaseT balun (the AVPro kit's single PSU plugs in at the rack TX and powers both ends over the cable; pre-wire rooms excluded), against the WattBox's outlets, with 20% (min 2) spare kept for the ISP modem and router. Advisor: switch out of ports, PoE device on a non-PoE switch, no LAN switch (sized: smallest Ubiquiti with spare), no power conditioner / outlets short / tight (sized: smallest WattBox 800 unit that fits). Every starter rack now includes a WattBox; the wire schedule gives every TV its own Cat6 network drop. Catalog: WattBox 800 / 800VPS / 820 (20A) / 700 / 300VB, from Snap One datasheets.
- **Routing audit.** Wires take the most efficient legal route under the drawing rules: top-row feeds choose the cheapest of the channel beside the rack, the corridor right of it, or a direct L (the far-right gutter only as a last resort — wires used to run to the sheet edge and double back); feeds to rooms right of the rack choose between diving under the band and rising beside the room; TV adapter chips are always reached from below; hairline kinks and redundant corners are cleaned up. `app/route-audit.js` measures every wire against the shortest legal route, and the tests hold the result as ceilings.
- **Hops and twists.** A wire crossing a bundle bridges it with one arc (the drafting convention); two wires that would wind round each other get their lanes or risers swapped so they stop crossing — roughly 60% fewer double crossings and a fifth fewer hops on dense sheets.
- **Tablet-ready.** On an iPad in landscape the top bar fits one row (the wordmark hides below 1,200px, the project name gives way first, no button ever wraps its label); below 1,000px it takes two rows. In portrait (≤900px wide) the drawing goes full width on top with the notes under it and the editor panel below — side by side left the sheet at a quarter scale. Phones work (no sideways scroll), just cramped.
- **Rack sheet fits one page** for a typical rack: its right-hand tables use compact 22px rows.
- **Advisor, two piles.** Under the drawing: what needs attention (errors, warnings, and the advisor notes that ask for a fix — missing controller, switch out of ports, outlets / circuit / heat, rack full…) always shows; everything else (setup checklists like the Dante switch settings, amp zone budgets, port counts, licensing, catalog updates) folds under one "Reference · N notes" line, open/closed remembered in this browser.
- **As-built mode.** File → **Start as-built** copies the job as "‹name› — As-Built" (stage AS-BUILT) and freezes the proposal inside it; the original job is never touched. Edit the copy to match the install — every difference from the proposal (rooms and their TVs/speakers, rack and in-room gear, adapters, wires) is found automatically, numbered, drawn as a **revision cloud with a △** on the sheet, and listed on the JOB tab. Removed items can't be clouded (they're not drawn any more) and are listed only. **Print ▾** then offers two packets: **Service** (every page, clouds on, an *As-Built Changes* page right after sheet 1) and **Client** (a clean drawing without marks, the equipment list and the rack — no ports, labels or change list). The AI review file carries the change list too. **Install record:** the JOB tab on an as-built job lists every box (rack gear, then in-room gear by room) with Serial, MAC, IP and Notes fields. MACs are tidied to `AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF` from any common spelling; a malformed IP/MAC or an address used on two boxes is flagged. It prints as an *Install Record* page in the service packet (blank rows double as a write-in form) and exports from **Export → Install record** (.csv). It's stored on each box (`install`), so it moves and deletes with the box, and it never counts as a change from the proposal. **Switch ports:** below it, each switch lists its boxes with a port field — blank shows the suggested port, typed ports (`12`, `sfp1`, `port 7` → `12`, `SFP 1`, `7`) are kept as patched and the suggestions for everything else route around them. Two boxes on one port are flagged. On the as-built Network page recorded ports print black, suggestions gray, and the table reads in port order. Stored as `solution.portMap[switch][box]`; not a △ change.
- **Zoom.** Pinch on the iPad (two fingers zoom *and* pan; one finger scrolls), trackpad pinch or ⌘/Ctrl-scroll on a Mac, or the − / % / + control at the sheet's top-right (tap the % to fit the sheet again). 100–600%; every zoom keeps the point under your fingers or pointer where it was, and it's a view setting — it survives edits and redraws, never touches the job or the print. A tap right after a pinch is ignored so lifting two fingers never selects a wire.
- **Top bar, grouped.** Job picker · **File ▾** (New job, Import…, Delete job) · project name · solution · stage · Undo · ✦ AI · rules chip · **View ▾** (Hide network, Harness style, Black & white) · **Export ▾** (PlanQueue, Takeoff CSV, Cable labels CSV · Job file JSON, Drawing SVG, AI review) · **Print** · TOOLS. Every menu item says what it makes; one menu open at a time, Escape or a click outside closes it; View's switches keep the menu open so you can flip several. (Delete moved off the bar into File, away from the job picker.)
- **RACK tab + Rack sheet — everything about the rack in one place.** The editor's RACK tab (beside ZONES / GEAR / JOB) shows, per rack: size, a live elevation, every box's rack units · cords · typical W · max W, the rack hardware with part numbers, and the power & heat totals with their advisor notes. Values from the catalog show gray; anything nobody has a figure for is **?** — type it in and it's saved on the job (on the device, or `job.rackParts` for hardware part numbers), overriding the catalog everywhere. The printed **Rack** sheet (one per rack) carries the same: elevation left; summary, rack hardware (unknown part numbers print ?) and the power & heat table right. The **Network** sheet is now switch ports only. Saved jobs keep their locked catalog values but pick up fields their locked copy never had (heights, watts), so old jobs get the new data without shifting a quote.
- **Rack Elevation sheet** (on by default; JOB → Rack size, default 42U). A front view of each rack in Synergy build order: Cat6 patch panels (one 24-port per 24 home runs, counted from the wire schedule) at the top, then network, control/processing, sources and small boxes on 2U shelves (up to 3 each), and receivers + amps on the floor with a 1U vent under each, the WattBox at the bottom. Half-width amps pair up; vertical power strips ride the rear rails. Heights come from the catalog (`rackUnits`); unknown ones draw dashed as 1U and are listed. Advisor: rack over-full, heights to confirm.
- **Circuit load.** The outlet budget now sums watts per rack box — typical (1/8 power, what music really draws) and max — against the WattBox circuit's continuous rating (80% of 15A = 1440 W, 20A for the 820 series, one circuit per WattBox). A PoE switch counts its own electronics plus the PoE gear it actually powers, not its whole PoE budget. Advisor: typical over the circuit → split circuits or a 20A WB-820; peak over → dedicated 20A circuit for the amps. Wattage and rack heights researched from manufacturer datasheets for the amps, receivers, matrices, switches and control gear; Anthem MDX and Hyperion publish no draw (shown "?", so the total is a floor).
- **Rack heat + sizing profiles.** The power budget converts typical load to heat (BTU/hr = W × 3.412); past ~500 W the advisor asks for a top-exhaust rack fan, past ~1,500 W for room cooling (rules of thumb, and it says so). Unlinked sources are sized from catalog *profiles* matched by name — "Apple TV 2" → Apple TV 4K (3rd gen), "Cable Box" → a provider planning figure (the highest published cable-DVR draw), DIRECTV / Xfinity / Spectrum boxes, Roku, Kaleidescape — for watts, rack space and Ethernet only; the quote exports still read the job's own links, so provider boxes stay provider equipment. Rack-side MXNet endpoints sit in AVPro's own rack kits on the elevation (AC-MXNET-1G-R2 / -R15, AC-MXNET-10G-HDRACK), picked for the fewest rack units; Dante encoders, which AVPro says don't fit the 1G racks, ride a shelf.
- **Cable labels.** One label per wire, two copies (rack end + field end), numbered like the wire schedule; speaker runs split per wire (S-05 Family Room FL … SR, L/R, SAT 1–8). **Export ▾ → Cable labels** saves a CSV for Brady Workstation / P-touch Editor; JOB → Packet pages → Cable Labels prints a cut sheet.
- **Dante distribution.** A job's audio network is Dante *or* Savant AVB (JOB → Audio network), separate from the control platform. The **Dante (Director amps)** starting rack and quick-add wire it the Synergy way: MXNet TVs get a **DANTE-DV2** decoder (sources get DANTE-EV2 encoders) — audio follows video; TVs without MXNet get an **AXIS2** on their eARC; surround rooms get an **AXIS16** into a **Hyperion** Penta (5.1) / Hepta (7.1), or onto a **Director** across 4–5 zones with the word "director"; stereo zones take the next Director outputs. Every Dante job gets a Dante-mode CBOX-HA (a second one beside the MXNet CBOX) and a dedicated SW24E. Dante links draw dashed; the wire list prints each AXIS's Cat6 to the Dante switch. The advisor covers: one CBOX per MXNet platform (1G / 10G / USP / Dante), DANTE-DV2 carries the source's audio not the TV's apps, VLAN 99 trunking, AXIS2 → set the TV to PCM, multicast when a small encoder feeds 3+ amps, and the Dante switch checklist.
- **Catalog stays current in every browser.** A catalog entry you never edited follows the shipped specs automatically; one you edited is kept and listed in ⚙ Settings as "differs from the latest shipped specs" with an Update button. All 55 entries were re-audited against manufacturer spec sheets on 2026-09-27 (see RESEARCH-CATALOG, round 5).
- **AVWalk → SignalPath in one tap.** AVWalk's OUTPUT tab has a SignalPath card: **Open** (same device) or **Send link** (AirDrop it to the Mac). The survey rides in the link's `#avwalk=` fragment (raw-DEFLATE + base64url, ~2 KB for a 10-room house), never reaches a server, and is cleared from the address bar on arrival. It lands on the import review, where **Draw the rack + wiring** (on by default) turns the walk into a first-draft schematic: MXNet switch (sized by port count) + encoder per rack source + decoder per on-system TV; multi-zone amps for house-fed speakers (behind Savant AVB on a Savant job); a receiver per 5.1/7.1 room or passive soundbar; powered soundbars off the TV's eARC; local amps for locally-fed rooms. Rooms that share a walk audio-zone label stay separate rooms with their own outputs on the same amp. Anything it can't decide (no-feed TVs, direct feeds, in-room receivers) comes back as a ⚠ line.
- **Harness bundling:** when a corridor is too crowded for every wire to get its own lane, feeds from the same device travel as one heavier run with a ×N count and break out near their destinations, electrical-drawing style (each wire still traces individually). Tick **View ▾ → Harness style** to draw every busy sheet this way on purpose: any source with 3+ runs of one signal to rooms shares a trunk (saved with the job, so print and export match).
- **Catalog lock:** each job freezes the device specs it was quoted with; the advisor flags when the catalog has newer data and JOB → Update catalog specs pulls it in.
- **HDBaseT balun = AVPro Edge AC-EX70-444-KIT** (Ryan 2026-09-29). Every balun quotes to PlanQueue by that SKU (18Gbps, 4K60 4:4:4 HDR, TX+RX, PoH). The kit carries **no ARC/eARC**, so a receiver feeding a TV through one defaults to **eARC + optical backup** (Ryan 2026-09-29) — the optical run is what brings the TV's own apps back. Choosing "eARC only" under TV audio back drops the run and brings back the warning; re-picking the same feed keeps that choice.
- **2.1 on a multi-zone amp** (Ryan 2026-09-29): two powered channels for the pair; the sub is fed from that zone's sub out (line level, MDX-16 style) to a powered sub — the wire schedule names it ("MDX-16 sub out 2 (zone 2)"), and an amp with fewer sub outs than 2.1 zones is flagged (only where the catalog lists its sub outs).
- **Audio Setup sheet** (on by default; JOB → Packet pages). Follows every source's audio through the drawing — matrix, MXNet, baluns, receivers — and applies one rule set (Ryan 2026-09-29): surround rooms (5.1+) decode bitstream, so their sources stay on **Auto**; a 2-channel box (MDX / Director digital ins, analog ins, AXIS2, Savant input module) only plays 2ch PCM, so something on the way must downmix — an **AXION**'s audio outs, an **AC-AVDM**, a **receiver** — and where nothing can, the **source is set to Stereo** ("downres the source, keep it simple"), printed with the exact menu path (Apple TV, Roku, DirecTV, Xfinity/Spectrum). The MX-88/44/1616 don't downmix; MXNet EVO II encoders/decoders (incl. the Dante models) DO — their 5-pin out is "downmixed to PCM 2Ch" per AVPro's spec sheet, and their Dante stream is taken to be the same 2ch PCM (Dante carries PCM only; the sheet doesn't state it); a cable box's own analog out is already stereo. When a downres'd source also feeds a surround room, the sheet and the advisor warn. TVs: sound back to a receiver or AXIS16 = Auto; into anything else = PCM / Stereo. Port facts come from a 2026-09-29 rear-panel audit (AXION: every AUDIO block always downmixes, HDMI/HDBaseT keep full; MRX: only HDMI OUT 1 is eARC). `app/audiochain.js`.
- **Ports — every wire lands on a real jack** (`app/ports.js`). Audited gear carries its rear-panel port list in the catalog (`ports`, from the 2026-09-29 audit of 34 products — raw data in `research/ports-*.json`); other catalog gear gets ports from its input/output counts, and unlinked gear by type (an unlinked "Apple TV" jacks like the Apple TV profile: HDMI only). Each wire gets a free, compatible jack at both ends — a receiver's HDMI OUT 1 (eARC) for its TV, each amp zone's own speaker terminals, a receiver's Zone 2 out into a house amp, analog before a PCM-only digital input, an MXNet encoder's LAN jack (the switch port is the Network sheet's). A wire with no free jack (a cable box's one HDMI out feeding two places) is flagged in the advisor and on the box. **On paper:** a **Patch List** sheet (on by default) lists every connection jack to jack with its cable, rack patching first; the wire schedule's rack end names the jack ("Rack · Anthem MDX-16 ZONE 1 SPEAKER").
- **One picker for every long choice** — a searchable sheet (bottom sheet on the iPad). **Product:** your go-to gear of that kind first (MRX 540/740/1140, MDX-16/8, Director M6800D, AC-MX-88, AXION-8, SW24E, AC-EX70-444-KIT, WattBox 800, Apple TV, Roku), then the rest of that kind; legacy gear and other kinds of box only while searching. **＋ Plug something into… / Connect an output:** every other box with the jack pair the wire would use ("Anthem MRX 740 — ZONE 2 OUT L/R · into MDX-8 ANALOG 2"); boxes that can't connect are **grayed with the reason** (Ryan 2026-09-29: gray, don't hide) — "no matching jacks — Apple TV: HDMI out; MDX-8: analog / optical in". **Advanced** keeps the free-form wire for the exceptions. **Jack chips** on every wire: tap to pick the port (used and wrong-kind jacks grayed with what's on them); a hand-picked jack shows 📌 and always wins.
- **The drawing grows with the job** (2026-09-30): the sheet's drawing scales to fill the page both ways — a big job shrinks to fit as before, and a small one now grows (up to 160%) so its rooms, boxes and captions print larger instead of sitting small in a corner; spare width is split evenly left and right. **Placement uses the page width:** the layout rules stay (surround + TV rooms along the top, TV-only rooms in the mid band, speaker rooms bottom-right, rack at the left), but each job is laid out a few ways — the top row allowed to run longer when the sheet will be scaled down anyway, and the TV-only rooms beside the speaker rooms instead of stacked on them — and the one that prints biggest wins (by a 3% margin, so a job that already fills the width keeps its classic layout). On 60 random jobs: 8.3% bigger on average, never smaller, and the wiring got cleaner too (fewer detours, twists and best-effort wires). `place(…, { classicLayout: true })` gives the old layout.
- **UI refresh (2026-09-30, from the before/after mockup Ryan approved).** One control per kind of choice: a few options → chips (the six most-used speaker setups plus **More**; TV sizes plus **Other**; three-way choices as segmented controls), anything longer → a field button that opens the picker sheet (every remaining menu in the panel is converted automatically; the hidden `<select>` still does the work), text → an input. One meaning per colour: filled blue = selected, outlined = action (＋ Plug something in), soft blue ✦ = suggestion (with Apply), amber / red = to check, green = done; jacks are quiet gray tags. Labels sit above fields; touch devices get 44 px targets. **Top bar** in three groups — one **job button** (name, stage · option; opens rename, stage, option, New / Import / Start as-built / Delete and other jobs), **Ask AI** and the status badge, then View · Export · **Print** (primary) · tools icon. **Advisor** is one strip — "⚠ N to check · M notes" and the first item — that opens into a list: short title, one line of detail, and **Go to ›** to the room or box it names; on a phone the list is a full-width sheet.
- **Zone editor details:** a TV **Model** field sits under Brand — a model typed there is quoted to PlanQueue by name instead of "Unspecified TV". A soundbar zone's Speakers picker offers "The TV — powered bar on HDMI eARC". Changing a room's scope (pre-wire ↔ included) moves its runs with it, and the advisor flags an Included room still fed by Pre-wire runs (it wouldn't be quoted or scheduled live). An amp, receiver or matrix that drives something with nothing plugged into it is flagged. "＋ Plug something into…" on GEAR starts with a sensible input: the matrix for a receiver, the audio module / Dante bridge / a source for an amp — never the power strip. A small rack box (cable box, Apple TV) with three or more wires, at least one to another rack box, draws taller so every exit gets its own lane.
- **One plain-English vocabulary:** every name a person reads — editor, advisor, sheet, print pages, CSV, quick-add, import review — comes from `app/names.js`, so a box is called the same thing everywhere ("HDMI matrix", "HDBaseT balun at Family Room TV", "Audio return (TV → rack)"). Connections read as sentences grouped by the box the signal leaves; the From/To pickers are grouped (in the rack / adapters / in-zone gear / in the zones), and re-pointing a connection re-guesses what it carries. No raw ids on screen or paper — a test enforces it. A box linked to a catalog product is called by that product's name (brand + model, part numbers shown beside it in the picker, not in the name): picking a product renames the box, and any box whose name drifted from its product is flagged (●) with a one-click fix. Long names are squeezed to fit their rack tile; two-word brands (AVPro Edge) stay together.
- **Interactive sheet:** tap a wire to trace it end-to-end; its tooltip offers **Reroute…**, **Edit connection** (opens that exact connection in GEAR → Connections to change its ends, signal, outputs or scope — the wire stays highlighted and follows the edit) and, for anything serving a zone, **<Zone> hookup**. Opening a connection row in the panel highlights its wire on the sheet. Tap a zone card or device tile to jump to its editor row.
- **Guided rerouting:** don't like a wire's path? Tap it → Reroute → pick from ghost previews of every legal alternate (with live crossing counts). Works on returns, rack-to-rack runs, and feeds out to rooms (riser channel, which card edge it lands on, and in/out of the harness). Choices are stored as topological hints, so they survive re-layout and re-import.
- **Autosave** to IndexedDB (with an in-memory fallback and a warning when browser storage is unavailable); native JSON and Markdown round-trip import/export; SVG export.
- **Hardened against hostile input:** every user string is escaped before it reaches the SVG or the editor DOM, imported JSON is structurally validated and stripped of prototype-pollution keys before it can replace a job, and CSV exports neutralize spreadsheet formula injection.

## Running it

**Easiest:** open the live URL above. It works offline-ish after first load and runs fine on an iPad. Right after an update, a browser still holding old files shows *"SignalPath was just updated — Reload"* instead of a blank page; your jobs are kept either way.

**Locally:** the app uses ES module imports, so it needs to be served over HTTP (double-clicking `index.html` will hit CORS restrictions in most browsers). Any static server works:

```bash
cd signalpath && python3 -m http.server 8745
```

Then open http://localhost:8745/. Load a sample job from the jobs dropdown (Residence / Estate / Stress fixtures ship with the app).

## Tests

Open `app/tests.html` in a served browser tab. 671 assertions run on load and report pass/fail with a summary line. The suite covers the data model, validation, advisor budgets, placement geometry, router hop ceilings per fixture (regression guards), render output, importers, quick-add parsing, catalog integrity, and print pages. **All tests green is the bar for every change.** `app/fuzz.html` complements it: hand-built edge cases plus hundreds of seeded random jobs pushed through the whole pipeline, checking for crashes, malformed SVG, `undefined`/`NaN` on paper, wires crossing any device or card body, overlapping hop arcs, vanished wires, and whether a picked reroute (returns, rack runs and zone feeds) reproduces its preview and keeps the whole sheet legal. Every job is also routed in harness style and with outdoor zones as their own cluster, held to the same geometry checks. Router fallbacks on overloaded layouts are reported as a capacity metric, not a failure.

## Architecture in one paragraph

The engine is a chain of pure functions with no DOM access: `loadJob` normalizes and indexes the job, `validate`/`advise` report problems and capacity budgets, `place` computes every box's geometry, `route` turns connections into channel-routed polylines, and `render` emits the finished SVG string. The UI in `index.html` is a thin shell that re-runs the pipeline on every edit and injects the SVG. Because the engine is headless, the entire behavior is testable from `tests.html` without a browser UI, and print/export are just the same render call with different options.
