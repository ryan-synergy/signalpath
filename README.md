# SignalPath

**CAD-style AV system schematics in the browser.** SignalPath turns a room-by-room description of a residential AV job into an 11×17 engineering sheet — equipment rack, zone cards, signal wiring with hop arcs, title block — plus channel map, takeoff, wire schedule, and BOM comparison pages. Built for [Synergy Audio Video Systems](https://github.com/ryan-synergy) as part of the Synergy Field Kit.

**Live app:** https://ryan-synergy.github.io/signalpath/

No build step, no dependencies, no server. Pure static HTML + ES modules.

---

## What it does

- **Draw a full system schematic** from structured job data: sources, matrices, amps, and switches in a three-column rack; per-room zone cards with display/speaker glyphs; wires routed through a structured channel router with crossing hops, trunk-run bus ticks (`×N`), and a dynamic legend.
- **Five signal types**, color-coded (and dash-coded in grayscale/print mode): video, audio, audio return, network, pre-wire.
- **Multiple solutions per job** (e.g. "Savant AVB" vs "Dante" bids) with per-solution endpoint overrides, a solution-aware advisor, and a side-by-side BOM comparison page.
- **A verified device catalog** (55+ SKUs — Savant, Sonance, Anthem, AudioControl, AVPro Edge, Sonos) with typed I/O counts sourced from manufacturer datasheets. The advisor checks port budgets (analog ins, module outs, digital return ins), licensing tiers, and gear-specific gotchas (AVB switch requirements, Sonos lip-sync buffering).
- **Print pages:** channel map (per-device port tables), takeoff (NEW/OFE rollup + CSV export; adapters, Bullet Train runs and eARC kits listed by the same AVPro part numbers the PlanQueue file quotes), wire schedule (numbered runs with cable totals; on a job with more than one rack each run starts at the rack it really comes from — a pool house's runs from the pool house rack — and the link between racks is its own run, **X-01** "Rack interconnect", Cat6 / fiber, with a distance check), BOM compare. Long jobs paginate onto continuation sheets with true "Sheet N of M" numbering. One-click print to 17×11 landscape.
- **Tabs in build order (2026-10-02): Rooms · Rack · Paperwork · ⚙.** Rooms is what used to be Zones. Rack is one place per rack: its boxes (each box's product, inputs, outputs — formerly GEAR) and, folded under one line, the elevation, heights / cords / watts, rack hardware and power & heat. Paperwork holds the client, solution, packet pages, catalog specs and revisions, with every connection (searchable) under Advanced at the bottom. Old links (a tapped box, "All connections") land in the new places.
- **New job: simple cards (2026-10-02).** Kits are grouped — Your kits · Whole home · Smaller jobs · One room · Start from — and each card is a small rack picture in the equipment colours (MXNet magenta, amps blue, receiver purple, network green, sources black), the kit's name, one line (`line` in starters.js; your own kits use their first sentence) and its size ("8 boxes · 4 starting rooms"). The full description, part list and starting rooms sit behind **Details**. Your kit-manager order holds inside each group.
- **Synergy's minimum cabinet + gear depth (2026-10-02).** Every rack states what its cabinet / closet needs: at least **22" wide × 26" deep** (Synergy's minimum), never less than the rack itself, plus its height on casters + 1". An opening entered under 22" × 26" is flagged. Racked gear carries its makers' published chassis depth (49 products, each with its source in the catalog — Anthem MRX taken at 15.0" counting knob and connectors, MDX at 17.0" with its terminals); with a rack picked, the deepest box is checked against the rack's usable depth — too deep, or under 3" left behind it for plugs and cables, is flagged (the MDX-16 in Strong's FS 42U, 18.25" usable, is tight). **Fit the space** now takes the tallest rack that fits *and* is deep enough, and marks shallow ones.
- **Works offline on a job site (2026-10-02).** The app keeps a copy of itself on the device (a service worker, `sw.js`): after one visit with signal it opens with none, and picks up a new version whenever there's signal. Jobs were already saved on the device.
- **Three ways to size a rack (2026-10-02).** Each rack on the Rack tab has a **Rack size** switch: **Fit the space** (type the opening — the tallest Middle Atlantic / Strong rack that fits is used, or tap another that fits), **Pick a rack** (any Middle Atlantic / Strong model and depth from a list, whatever the space — it still warns if it won't fit a space you entered), or **By U** (a plain count, no brand). Each mode keeps its own inputs, so switching back and forth loses nothing; "By U" starts from the size the rack is now. Older jobs open in the mode that matches what they have (a model → Pick a rack, a space → Fit the space, neither → By U).
- **Start from the space (2026-10-02).** Racks are usually limited by the cabinet opening or the door they go through, so each rack on the Rack tab has **Space for this rack** (height, width, depth in inches — the lower of the opening and the door). It lists every Middle Atlantic / Strong floor rack that fits (heights on casters, tallest first, one exact part number per depth) and picks the tallest; one tap picks another. The picked rack sets the U count, goes on the hardware list by part number, and the elevation and quick reference follow. If the gear doesn't fit: **Squeeze the spacing** (only the vents round receivers and amps stay — routers and switches stack), **Add a second rack beside it** (same space and rack; wiring between the two is patching, not a run, and it shares the first rack's sheet) and drag gear onto it on the rack page, or a taller rack when one still fits. A picked rack that doesn't fit the space is flagged with the one that does. The clearance over the rack is a 1" placeholder (`RACK_CLEARANCE` in racksizes.js) until Synergy's minimum-cabinet sheet is added.
- **Schematic · Rack — two views of the same job (2026-10-02).** A switch in the top bar flips the big area between the schematic (wiring) and the racks full size (fine-tuning); the panel on the left stays as it is, so the mini rack and the space fields sit beside the big racks and every change shows in both at once. The app remembers which view you left it in; Esc goes back to the schematic.
- **The rack view — drag to arrange (2026-10-02).** Flip the top-bar switch to Rack (or tap a rack on the Rack tab, or its name on the drawing) and every rack of the solution shows side by side, to scale. Drag a box up or down and it snaps to whole U; drop it on a box the same height and they swap; drop it on anything else and what was there moves to the nearest free space. Drag it onto another rack and it moves there for real — its wiring, power load and patch counts follow (a shelf takes its boxes along; an MXNet encoder kit stays with its sources, a patch panel with its runs). Vents and brush plates fill in around the arrangement; a spacing rule it breaks (an amp with no vent above) becomes a warning on the rack page and in the advisor, not a block. The first move freezes that rack's layout (saved on the job as `rack.layout`, rows from the top), new gear drops into the first free space, and **Reset to auto** returns to the build-order stack. Undo works for every move; the printed rack page draws the arrangement.
- **Rooms wire themselves (2026-10-02).** Pick a rack kit and it draws at once; type rooms in plain words (`family room 5.1 75 sony, kitchen stereo, patio landscape`) and the preview shows, room by room, what will feed each one before you press Add (`TV ← AC-MXNET-SW8P (decoder) · speakers ← new Anthem MRX 540`). On a whole-home rack every TV is fed from the matrix / MXNet switch — no `matrix` keyword (still accepted); `local` puts an Apple TV at the TV, `apps` means the TV plays its own apps (no rack feed, not an error — also "TV's own apps" in the room's TV video menu). A surround room that's part of the job gets its own Anthem receiver automatically (5.1 → MRX 540, 7.1 → 740, Atmos → 1140) unless it's a Dante job or a pre-wire room. An amp given speakers on an MXNet rack gets the MXNet audio decoder that feeds it. Every kit plus plain rooms is held to zero errors by a test.
- **Tap to finish a room; say it any way (2026-10-02).** While you type a room, the preview offers what it still needs as big tappable bubbles — Speakers: Stereo · 5.1 · 7.1 · Atmos · Soundbar · Landscape · No speakers; TV: 55″ · 65″ · 75″ · 85″ · Projector · No TV; then ＋ Next room — so "master bedroom" + two taps is a finished room. The reader takes plain English: `65 inch` / `65 inches` / `65-inch tv` / `65 in.` / `65"`; `two channel` / `2 channel` / `2ch` / `stereo pair` (stereo); `five channel` / `surround` (5.1); `atmos` / `dolby atmos` / `7.2.4` (7.1.4); spelled-out numbers (`sixty five inch`, `landscape eight`, `four speakers`, `projector one twenty`); `4 ceiling speakers`; and "tv" is never left in a room's name. **Dictation-proof** (Wispr Flow writes full sentences): commas, periods, semicolons and "then" split clauses, and a clause that names no room ("65-inch TV", "two-channel stereo", "with a 65") belongs to the room before it — so "Master bedroom, 65-inch TV, two-channel stereo." is one room and "The kitchen is stereo only, no TV." is a kitchen; sentence words ("has", "is", "with", "only", "audio"…) never become part of a name; a setup it doesn't know (3.1) is flagged, not named.
- **One count, one-click fixes.** The top-bar chip and the bottom strip show the same "N to check" (the chip opens the list). Problems the app knows how to fix carry buttons — in the list and on the room or box itself: an unfed TV → "Feed it from the AC-MXNET-SW8P" / "Add an Apple TV at the TV" / "It plays its own apps"; unfed speakers → the right receiver, "Use the Anthem MDX-16" (next free zone) or "Add an Anthem MDX-16"; an amp with no source → its MXNet audio decoder, or the receiver's line out; no LAN switch / no or too-small WattBox → add (or swap in) exactly what the advisor sized, in the right rack; a switch out of ports → "Step up to" the smallest switch of its kind that fits (same MXNet generation; its connections stay); a receiver nothing plays into → a rack source or "Add an Apple TV in the rack"; a surround TV whose apps can't reach the speakers → optical back or the eARC kit. (A projector theater isn't told about TV apps — a projector has none.) Each fix is one undo step.
- **Where an encoder's audio goes (2026-10-02).** A source's MXNet AVDM encoder breaks the source's audio out as analog — normally into the Savant input module. On the Rack tab, the source's row has **Encoder audio out**: the input module (default), straight to an amp or a receiver (bypassing the AIM), gear in a room (a local amp — it becomes a line-level **A** run on the wire schedule), or not used. An amp's MXNet audio decoder has the same picker. **＋ New local amp in <room>** puts an amp in that room for the rare room feed (it drives the room's speakers if nothing does yet). The drawing, port budgets and paperwork follow.
- **A lighter room editor.** An open room shows its setup (speakers, display, size, brand) and what feeds the TV and speakers; how they're fed — run type, distance, amp outputs, TV audio back, Dante — folds under one line that reads like a summary ("MXNet decoder · average run · eARC") and opens to change it. Model, name, scope, status and remote are under More. The "feed the TV from the receiver too" suggestion only appears when nothing feeds the TV (separate feeds from the matrix are the practice).
- **Click the drawing to edit.** Every part of the sheet opens where it's edited: a room → its row; a rack box → the Rack tab; an adapter chip → the room it serves (a DEC / balun at a TV) or the box (an ENC on a source, an amp's audio decoder); a rack's name → the RACK tab at that rack; the title block → JOB at the client name; a wire → its trace, with reroute / edit / hookup. Things you can click highlight on hover.
- **Quick-add / dictation entry:** type or speak `family room 5.1 75 sony matrix, patio landscape 8 prewire` and get parsed zones with preview chips. Add `avr` (or `receiver`) and the zone comes wired: an AV receiver in the rack feeds the TV (through an HDBaseT balun) and drives the speakers — the Anthem that fits the room (5.1 → MRX 540, 7.1 → MRX 740, 7.1.4 → MRX 1140; pick another on the Rack tab), fed from the matrix on a whole-home rack; `matrix` feeds the TV from the rack's HDMI matrix; `local` puts an Apple TV at the TV and wires it; a powered soundbar is fed by its TV over eARC; a `prewire` TV gets its pre-wire run back to the matrix. A room with nothing in it ("bonus room future") is flagged in the preview and skipped with a note, rather than silently dropped. It takes dictation as people talk: "kitchen 55 no wait 65" keeps the 65, "den same as the office" copies the office's gear, "two bedrooms 50 stereo" makes Bedroom 1 and 2, "conference room and training room both 65" gives both the TV, "upstairs master" is just Master but "upper bedroom, lower bedroom" keep their floors (a floor stays only when two rooms need telling apart), "no audio" means no speakers, and "uh / um / okay so / don't forget" never land in a name. "Surround sound" is a 5.1 (with Atmos, a 7.1.4). Things said about a room — "with an Apple TV", "and the Sonos", "a sub", "over the fireplace" — become its note (a sub turns a soundbar into soundbar + sub), never a room of their own. Typing a room the job already has changes it ("family room surround sound", "the kitchen, 65 inch Samsung", "master bed 55") instead of adding a second one; a line can change one room and add another. `7.1.4` reads "7.1.4 Atmos" everywhere and has its own button in the zone editor.
- **The side panel, zone- and box-first:**
  - *ZONES*: each row reads closed — a ✓ / ! mark, what's in the zone and what feeds it ("5.1 surround + 75\" TV · TV ← Axion 8 · speakers ← MRX 540 8K · eARC"), and the advisor's warning for that zone right on the row. Open, the essentials are taps (speaker setup, TV/projector, size presets), then Hookup; name, scope, status, remote and the size check sit behind **More**.
  - *the Rack tab*: each box is its back panel — **Inputs** and **Outputs** listing what's plugged in (each editable in place), jack use against the catalog ("HDMI 2 of 7", "zones 6 of 8", matching the advisor), and ＋ buttons to plug something in or connect an output.
  - *Paperwork → Advanced → All connections*: the full list with search, for checking — no longer the main way to wire.
  - Tapping a zone, box or wire on the drawing focuses just that item in the panel; tapping a box also lights up every wire it has. A receiver's tile names the room it drives ("Loft · Anthem MRX 540"), and so does every list of receivers. The panel is resizable (drag its edge; remembered per browser).
- **Commands (type what you want):** the quick-add box also takes changes — "connect cable box to mrx", "kitchen, office and dining to amp 2", "family room from the receiver", "master bed tv direct hdmi", "master optical backup", "patio 75 ofe", "gym prewire", "rename kitchen to Chef's Kitchen", "add an mrx 1140", "delete the turn table" (several: separate with ";"). It previews exactly what will happen before Enter; **↶ Undo** (or ⌘Z) takes back the last command or AI change.
- **✦ AI — Claude drives it:** describe a change in plain words. Claude proposes it as commands; the app runs them on a copy, checks the result with the validator, and shows a preview (each step ✓/✗, problems before → after) that you apply or discard — nothing changes by itself. Three ways in:
  - *Copy prompt for Claude* → paste into claude.ai → paste the reply back. No setup, no key.
  - *Ask Claude* built in: ⚙ Settings → AI → your own API key (stored in this browser only), or a **proxy** that holds the key for the whole crew (`proxy/worker.js`, a Cloudflare Worker — deploy steps are in its header). The built-in AI gets the validator's verdict back and corrects itself (up to two passes) before you see the preview.
  - ✦ AI can also add racks, move gear between them, set a rack's size mode (locked to a space, auto, fixed U, casters), choose PoE power (injector / local supply) and route an encoder's audio.
  - *Check against the quote*: give it the original quote or system design (PDF, PlanQueue / CSV / text). Claude compares it with the job and proposes the missing gear (control box, injectors, amps…) as a preview; it never removes anything. Text quotes have their prices taken out before sending; a PDF goes as it is. The quote is never saved.
  - *For an outside agent* (Claude in Chrome, Claude Code): `window.SignalPath.summary() / vocabulary() / issues() / preview(cmds) / apply(cmds) / undo()`.
- **Rack starting points:** ＋ (new job) offers Savant whole-home (sets the job to Savant + AVB audio, so Savant host/licensing advice follows; the music server rides the AVB network — it's digital-only, and outside a Savant audio system it goes optical into the amp), Theater receiver, AV-over-IP (MXNet) or a blank rack — gear catalog-linked, the patching inside the rack done — and the quick-add box suggests the zones line for it. Quick-add then wires each zone: `matrix` feeds the TV, stereo-type speakers take the amp's next free outputs, surround gets a receiver fed from the matrix, `avr` gives the zone its own receiver.
- **Zone hookup:** every zone row has a HOOKUP section — *TV video* from (receiver, matrix, AV-over-IP switch, splitter, source, or gear in the zone), *Run to TV* (HDBaseT balun / MXNet decoder / direct HDMI), *Speakers* from (receiver or amp — an amp gets its next free outputs automatically), *TV audio back* — **eARC over the HDMI** (the default whenever a receiver feeds the TV; no extra run), **eARC + optical backup** (adds a Toslink run to the receiver), **optical only** (to a receiver or audio input module), or none. eARC shows on the channel map and on the video run in the wire schedule; only optical adds a pulled run. When eARC comes back through an HDBaseT balun or MXNet decoder, the advisor asks you to confirm that model passes eARC (many only pass ARC) until the optical backup is added. "＋ New AV receiver…" / "＋ New multi-zone amp…" add the box in place, and when a receiver already does half the zone a one-click button offers the other half. It all writes ordinary connections, visible in Rack → the box's Connections.
- **Rack size, three ways** (Rack tab): *Locked to space* — enter the opening, the tallest Middle Atlantic / Strong rack that fits is locked in and never grows (it squeezes the spacing first, then offers a second rack beside it); *Auto* — new builds size themselves to the gear and become two racks side by side past 42U (receivers and amps in the second), folding back when they fit again; *Fixed U* — type it, checked against the space if one is entered. A Casters / No casters toggle sits by the space fields, and the Auto two-wide limit (42U by default) is a field in the Auto panel. Half-width boxes (Savant AIM / AOM) share a row and each can be dragged into the empty half of another; low-heat boxes stack with no vent and can tuck into a vent space with a note.
- **Three pages: Schematic · Rack · As-built** (top bar — Ryan 2026-10-03). **As-built** on an as-built job is one page: the drawing as installed with changes in revision clouds, the numbered list of what changed from the proposal, and the install record as a table (serial, MAC, IP, notes per box — typed right there, MACs tidy themselves, bad or duplicate addresses flagged), with Open the proposal · Download install record · Print service packet. On a proposal it opens that job's as-built, or offers Start as-built.
- **Jobs screen** (📁 Jobs, top left — Ryan 2026-10-02: "break it out — where we start jobs, maintain jobs"): a full page with **Start a job** (New job from a rack kit · Paste a design · Import a file · Open a sample) and **Your jobs**, newest first, searchable by job, client or address. Each job shows its stage, client, address, room count, options and when it was last edited, with Open · Rename · Duplicate · Download (the .json backup) · Delete; on a phone each job shows Open + More…. Deleting the open job moves to the most recently edited one. An as-built is filed under the proposal it was started from (indented, with its start date) and stays there after a rename; a proposal with no as-built yet has **Start as-built** right on its card.
- **Markdown import** (File → Import, or File → Paste Markdown…): a design written by Claude, ChatGPT or a tech — headings, room lists, tables, emoji bullets, outlines, YAML, shorthand lines or an email — comes in as rooms and rack gear, wired the way quick-add would, after a review screen that lists anything it couldn't read. A PlanQueue file comes back too (TV sizes flagged to confirm), and an AI review file with a ✦ AI command list after it applies those changes. Export → *Design brief for an AI* gives another AI the format and the catalog's product names. Prices in a pasted design are never read.
- **Importers** for SiteWalk survey JSON and Blueprinted (Savant config) exports, with non-destructive re-import merge. A Savant config comes in wired: MXNet encoders on their sources, AIM/AOM/AVB gear as what it is, each room's old receiver kept, and every room hooked up the way quick-add would. Savant's "MXnet" driver profile comes in as an MXNet switch to confirm plus its control box; a VIMAP8S (IP-video transmitter), AIO1C (AVB audio endpoint) and the rack's WattBox strips come in as what they are; speakers the config fed line-level (their amp not in it) get a stand-in amp flagged to confirm. A PoE box on a switch that can't power it gets a per-box pick on the Rack tab (PoE injector in the rack, or a local power supply); injectors count on the WattBox, the takeoff and the PlanQueue file.
- **Zone grouping (Paperwork → Group zones).** Default **by type** — the Synergy sheet: the **top band** starts with surround rooms with TVs, then TVs with 2-channel speakers or a soundbar, left to right, wrapping downward as the page fills; **TV-only rooms** sit in the **mid band** right of the rack, between the TV rows and the speaker-only rooms; **speaker-only rooms** fill the bottom-right, bottom-aligned with the rack (added order kept inside each group). Zones sit tight: 20px between cards, 24px between speaker rows; a wide card (a landscape array) spans whole grid cells instead of spreading every column apart. **By type, outdoor zones separate** draws outdoor rooms as their own OUTDOOR cluster (name-based — patio, pool, yard… — or landscape speakers; each zone's More → Outdoor overrides the guess). **In the order I added them** keeps the old behaviour. The same outdoor test puts rooms under Exterior in the PlanQueue export.
- **Markdown exports.** **PlanQueue** saves the proposal-import file exactly to Synergy's PlanQ format sheet (2026-09-28): a front matter block holding only `planqueue: proposal-import/1`, a `# Client — Address` title, `## Room` headers, and `- qty | Manufacturer | Model` lines — no notes, floors, tables or prose, and no mandatory lines (EWR, PM fee, materials are added in PlanQueue). Rack gear and TV-side adapters go by their **PlanQueue SKU** (catalog `partNo`, synced to Synergy's PlanQueue product export of 2026-09-28 — SKUs only, never its dealer prices; gear PlanQueue doesn't carry goes by its manufacturer order code and imports as a needs-SKU line). What notes used to say is said with PlanQueue's own items: owner-supplied and provider gear → **Customer-Supplied** TV / Speakers / Soundbar / Subwoofer / Equipment; speakers without a model → **Synergy · Unspecified Speakers** with the speaker count as the quantity (the sub on its own line); a TV without a model → **Synergy · Unspecified TV**; pre-wire rooms → Synergy's **Prewire - …** items (TV (Standard), 5.1 / 7.1 / 7.2.4 Atmos, Speaker Pair / Single, Landscape Speakers / Subwoofer, Passive Soundbar (LCR) when an amp drives the bar). A projector with no model quotes as Synergy **Unspecified Projector** (projectors are rare, so there's no catalog of them — type the model in the zone's Model field and it quotes as that brand and model; an owner's projector is Customer-Supplied). A second rack wired to the main one (a pool house, a guest house) adds one Synergy **Prewire - Rack Interconnect** in that rack's section. Outdoor rooms come last; a room name repeated on two floors carries its floor. A job with pre-wire-only rooms saves two files (systems + "— Pre-Wire"); future rooms stay out. **AI .md** saves a review file for an agent: rooms and how each is fed, rack usage, open items, advisor notes, licensing, the switch port plan + outlet budget, connections, wire list, the quote lines, the command vocabulary, and the full job as a JSON block — which imports straight back into SignalPath (import takes the last ```json fence).
- **Network & Power sheet** (Paperwork → Packet pages; on by default). Every switch gets a port plan — which box lands on which port, on which network: MXNet ports (DANTE-DV2/EV2 marked "MXNet + VLAN 99", with the VLAN 99 trunk to the Dante switch), the Dante switch (controller, Director/Hyperion amps, AXIS encoders, Dante bridges), AVB, and the **house LAN**: every TV, every networked rack box (sources, receivers, amps, matrix, control hosts, the WattBox itself, a CBOX's control port) and one uplink per AV network. PoE is shown per port against the switch's budget. Rack gear numbers from port 1, TVs by zone, uplinks on the SFP cages. With a LAN switch in more than one rack, each TV and box lands on the switch in the rack that serves it. Under it, the **rack outlet budget** (one per rack — a pool house rack is its own building, with its own WattBox, circuit and heat, so its boxes never borrow the main rack's outlets; adapters count in the rack they plug in at): one outlet per rack box (catalog `outlets` for dual-cord gear) except PoE gear its switch powers, plus one per rack-fed HDBaseT balun and one per eARC extender kit (the AVPro kit's single PSU plugs in at the rack TX and powers both ends over the cable; pre-wire rooms excluded), against the WattBox's outlets, with 20% (min 2) spare kept for the ISP modem and router. Advisor: switch out of ports, PoE device on a non-PoE switch, no LAN switch (sized: smallest Ubiquiti with spare), no power conditioner / outlets short / tight (sized: smallest WattBox 800 unit that fits). Every starter rack now includes a WattBox; the wire schedule gives every TV its own Cat6 network drop. Catalog: WattBox 800 / 800VPS / 820 (20A) / 700 / 300VB, from Snap One datasheets.
- **Routing audit.** Wires take the most efficient legal route under the drawing rules: top-row feeds choose the cheapest of the channel beside the rack, the corridor right of it, or a direct L (the far-right gutter only as a last resort — wires used to run to the sheet edge and double back); feeds to rooms right of the rack choose between diving under the band and rising beside the room; TV adapter chips are always reached from below; hairline kinks and redundant corners are cleaned up. `app/route-audit.js` measures every wire against the shortest legal route, and the tests hold the result as ceilings.
- **Hops and twists.** A wire crossing a bundle bridges it with one arc (the drafting convention); two wires that would wind round each other get their lanes or risers swapped so they stop crossing — roughly 60% fewer double crossings and a fifth fewer hops on dense sheets.
- **Tablet-ready.** On an iPad in landscape the top bar fits one row (the wordmark hides below 1,200px, the project name gives way first, no button ever wraps its label); below 1,000px it takes two rows. In portrait (≤900px wide) the drawing goes full width on top with the notes under it and the editor panel below — side by side left the sheet at a quarter scale. Phones work (no sideways scroll), just cramped.
- **Rack sheet fits one page** for a typical rack: its right-hand tables use compact 22px rows.
- **Advisor, two piles.** Under the drawing: what needs attention (errors, warnings, and the advisor notes that ask for a fix — missing controller, switch out of ports, outlets / circuit / heat, rack full…) always shows; everything else (setup checklists like the Dante switch settings, amp zone budgets, port counts, licensing, catalog updates) folds under one "Reference · N notes" line, open/closed remembered in this browser.
- **As-built mode.** File → **Start as-built** copies the job as "‹name› — As-Built" (stage AS-BUILT) and freezes the proposal inside it; the original job is never touched. Edit the copy to match the install — every difference from the proposal (rooms and their TVs/speakers, rack and in-room gear, adapters, wires) is found automatically, numbered, drawn as a **revision cloud with a △** on the sheet, and listed on the Paperwork tab. Removed items can't be clouded (they're not drawn any more) and are listed only. **Print ▾** then offers two packets: **Service** (every page, clouds on, an *As-Built Changes* page right after sheet 1) and **Client** (a clean drawing without marks, the equipment list and the rack — no ports, labels or change list). The AI review file carries the change list too. **Install record:** the Paperwork tab on an as-built job lists every box (rack gear, then in-room gear by room) with Serial, MAC, IP and Notes fields. MACs are tidied to `AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF` from any common spelling; a malformed IP/MAC or an address used on two boxes is flagged. It prints as an *Install Record* page in the service packet (blank rows double as a write-in form) and exports from **Export → Install record** (.csv). It's stored on each box (`install`), so it moves and deletes with the box, and it never counts as a change from the proposal. **Switch ports:** below it, each switch lists its boxes with a port field — blank shows the suggested port, typed ports (`12`, `sfp1`, `port 7` → `12`, `SFP 1`, `7`) are kept as patched and the suggestions for everything else route around them. - **2.1 on a multi-zone amp** (Ryan 2026-09-29): two powered channels for the pair; the sub is fed from that zone's sub out (line level, MDX-16 style) to a powered sub — the wire schedule names it ("MDX-16 sub out 2 (zone 2)"), and an amp with fewer sub outs than 2.1 zones is flagged (only where the catalog lists its sub outs).
- **Audio Setup sheet** (on by default; Paperwork → Packet pages). Follows every source's audio through the drawing — matrix, MXNet, baluns, receivers — and applies one rule set (Ryan 2026-09-29): surround rooms (5.1+) decode bitstream, so their sources stay on **Auto**; a 2-channel box (MDX / Director digital ins, analog ins, AXIS2, Savant input module) only plays 2ch PCM, so something on the way must downmix — an **AXION**'s audio outs, an **AC-AVDM**, a **receiver** — and where nothing can, the **source is set to Stereo** ("downres the source, keep it simple"), printed with the exact menu path (Apple TV, Roku, DirecTV, Xfinity/Spectrum). The MX-88/44/1616 don't downmix; MXNet EVO II encoders/decoders (incl. the Dante models) DO — their 5-pin out is "downmixed to PCM 2Ch" per AVPro's spec sheet, and their Dante stream is taken to be the same 2ch PCM (Dante carries PCM only; the sheet doesn't state it); a cable box's own analog out is already stereo. When a downres'd source also feeds a surround room, the sheet and the advisor warn. TVs: sound back to a receiver or AXIS16 = Auto; into anything else = PCM / Stereo. Port facts come from a 2026-09-29 rear-panel audit (AXION: every AUDIO block always downmixes, HDMI/HDBaseT keep full; MRX: only HDMI OUT 1 is eARC). `app/audiochain.js`.
- **Ports — every wire lands on a real jack** (`app/ports.js`). Audited gear carries its rear-panel port list in the catalog (`ports`, from the 2026-09-29 audit of 34 products — raw data in `research/ports-*.json`); other catalog gear gets ports from its input/output counts, and unlinked gear by type (an unlinked "Apple TV" jacks like the Apple TV profile: HDMI only). Each wire gets a free, compatible jack at both ends — a receiver's HDMI OUT 1 (eARC) for its TV, each amp zone's own speaker terminals, a receiver's Zone 2 out into a house amp, analog before a PCM-only digital input, an MXNet encoder's LAN jack (the switch port is the Network sheet's). A wire with no free jack (a cable box's one HDMI out feeding two places) is flagged in the advisor and on the box. **On paper:** a **Patch List** sheet (on by default) lists every connection jack to jack with its cable, rack patching first; the wire schedule's rack end names the jack ("Rack · Anthem MDX-16 ZONE 1 SPEAKER").
- **One picker for every long choice** — a searchable sheet (bottom sheet on the iPad). **Product:** your go-to gear of that kind first (MRX 540/740/1140, MDX-16/8, Director M6800D, AC-MX-88, AXION-8, SW24E, AC-EX70-444-KIT, WattBox 800, Apple TV, Roku), then the rest of that kind; legacy gear and other kinds of box only while searching. **＋ Plug something into… / Connect an output:** every other box with the jack pair the wire would use ("Anthem MRX 740 — ZONE 2 OUT L/R · into MDX-8 ANALOG 2"); boxes that can't connect are **grayed with the reason** (Ryan 2026-09-29: gray, don't hide) — "no matching jacks — Apple TV: HDMI out; MDX-8: analog / optical in". **Advanced** keeps the free-form wire for the exceptions. **Jack chips** on every wire: tap to pick the port (used and wrong-kind jacks grayed with what's on them); a hand-picked jack shows 📌 and always wins.
- **The drawing grows with the job** (2026-09-30): the sheet's drawing scales to fill the page both ways — a big job shrinks to fit as before, and a small one now grows (up to 160%) so its rooms, boxes and captions print larger instead of sitting small in a corner; spare width is split evenly left and right. **Placement uses the page width:** the layout rules stay (surround + TV rooms along the top, TV-only rooms in the mid band, speaker rooms bottom-right, rack at the left), but each job is laid out a few ways — the top row allowed to run longer when the sheet will be scaled down anyway, and the TV-only rooms beside the speaker rooms instead of stacked on them — and the one that prints biggest wins (by a 3% margin, so a job that already fills the width keeps its classic layout). On 60 random jobs: 8.3% bigger on average, never smaller, and the wiring got cleaner too (fewer detours, twists and best-effort wires). `place(…, { classicLayout: true })` gives the old layout.
- **UI refresh (2026-09-30, from the before/after mockup Ryan approved).** One control per kind of choice: a few options → chips (the six most-used speaker setups plus **More**; TV sizes plus **Other**; three-way choices as segmented controls), anything longer → a field button that opens the picker sheet (every remaining menu in the panel is converted automatically; the hidden `<select>` still does the work), text → an input. One meaning per colour: filled blue = selected, outlined = action (＋ Plug something in), soft blue ✦ = suggestion (with Apply), amber / red = to check, green = done; jacks are quiet gray tags. Labels sit above fields; touch devices get 44 px targets. **Top bar** in three groups — **📁 Jobs** and one **job button** (name, stage · option; opens rename, stage, option, Start as-built, the five most recent jobs and All jobs…), **Ask AI** and the status badge, then View · Export · **Print** (primary) · tools icon. **Advisor** is one strip — "⚠ N to check · M notes" and the first item — that opens into a list: short title, one line of detail, and **Go to ›** to the room or box it names; on a phone the list is a full-width sheet.
- **Zone editor details:** a TV **Model** field sits under Brand — a model typed there is quoted to PlanQueue by name instead of "Unspecified TV". A soundbar zone's Speakers picker offers "The TV — powered bar on HDMI eARC". Changing a room's scope (pre-wire ↔ included) moves its runs with it, and the advisor flags an Included room still fed by Pre-wire runs (it wouldn't be quoted or scheduled live). An amp, receiver or matrix that drives something with nothing plugged into it is flagged. "＋ Plug something into…" on the Rack tab starts with a sensible input: the matrix for a receiver, the audio module / Dante bridge / a source for an amp — never the power strip. A small rack box (cable box, Apple TV) with three or more wires, at least one to another rack box, draws taller so every exit gets its own lane.
- **One plain-English vocabulary:** every name a person reads — editor, advisor, sheet, print pages, CSV, quick-add, import review — comes from `app/names.js`, so a box is called the same thing everywhere ("HDMI matrix", "HDBaseT balun at Family Room TV", "Audio return (TV → rack)"). Connections read as sentences grouped by the box the signal leaves; the From/To pickers are grouped (in the rack / adapters / in-zone gear / in the zones), and re-pointing a connection re-guesses what it carries. No raw ids on screen or paper — a test enforces it. A box linked to a catalog product is called by that product's name (brand + model, part numbers shown beside it in the picker, not in the name): picking a product renames the box, and any box whose name drifted from its product is flagged (●) with a one-click fix. Long names are squeezed to fit their rack tile; two-word brands (AVPro Edge) stay together.
- **Interactive sheet:** tap a wire to trace it end-to-end; its tooltip offers **Reroute…**, **Edit connection** (opens that exact connection in Rack → the box's Connections to change its ends, signal, outputs or scope — the wire stays highlighted and follows the edit) and, for anything serving a zone, **<Zone> hookup**. Opening a connection row in the panel highlights its wire on the sheet. Tap a zone card or device tile to jump to its editor row.
- **Guided rerouting:** don't like a wire's path? Tap it → Reroute → pick from ghost previews of every legal alternate (with live crossing counts). Works on returns, rack-to-rack runs, and feeds out to rooms (riser channel, which card edge it lands on, and in/out of the harness). Choices are stored as topological hints, so they survive re-layout and re-import.
- **Autosave** to IndexedDB (with an in-memory fallback and a warning when browser storage is unavailable); native JSON and Markdown round-trip import/export; SVG export.
- **Hardened against hostile input:** every user string is escaped before it reaches the SVG or the editor DOM, imported JSON is structurally validated and stripped of prototype-pollution keys before it can replace a job, and CSV exports neutralize spreadsheet formula injection.

## No pricing — a hard rule

This repo is public (it serves the live site), so SignalPath carries **no pricing of any kind**: no dealer or sell prices, no list/MSRP/retail prices, no subscription fees, and nothing from Synergy's private PlanQueue product export. SKUs and product names are fine. A schematic doesn't need prices — Synergy prices in PlanQueue. `deploy.sh` runs `check-public.py` on the exact copy it's about to publish and stops on any money amount, fee, price field or PlanQueue-export content; a test in `app/tests.html` scans the catalog and every shipped module the same way.

**Private windows.** If the browser won't let SignalPath save (a private window, or site data turned off), the app still works for that tab and says so in a bar at the bottom, with a button that saves a job file — use Export → Job file to keep the work.

## Running it

**Easiest:** open the live URL above. It works offline-ish after first load and runs fine on an iPad. Right after an update, a browser still holding old files shows *"SignalPath was just updated — Reload"* instead of a blank page; your jobs are kept either way.

**Locally:** the app uses ES module imports, so it needs to be served over HTTP (double-clicking `index.html` will hit CORS restrictions in most browsers). Any static server works:

```bash
cd signalpath && python3 -m http.server 8745
```

Then open http://localhost:8745/. Load a sample from 📁 Jobs → Open a sample (Residence / Estate / a 30-room, 4-rack Bel Air estate / Stress fixtures ship with the app).

## Tests

Open `app/tests.html` in a served browser tab. 1,065 assertions run on load and report pass/fail with a summary line. The suite covers the data model, validation, advisor budgets, placement geometry, router hop ceilings per fixture (regression guards), render output, importers, quick-add parsing, catalog integrity, and print pages. **All tests green is the bar for every change.** `app/fuzz.html` complements it: hand-built edge cases plus hundreds of seeded random jobs pushed through the whole pipeline — every job also drawn in trunk mode — (with room distances, Bullet Train runs and eARC kits layered on from a second random stream, junk values included), checking for crashes, malformed SVG, two different wires sharing a lane, `undefined`/`NaN` on paper and in the text outputs (takeoff, wire runs, advisor, jack plan, PlanQueue file, AI review), wires crossing any device or card body, overlapping hop arcs, vanished wires, and whether a picked reroute (returns, rack runs and zone feeds) reproduces its preview and keeps the whole sheet legal. Every job is also routed in harness style and with outdoor zones as their own cluster, held to the same geometry checks. Router fallbacks on overloaded layouts are reported as a capacity metric, not a failure.

## Architecture in one paragraph

The engine is a chain of pure functions with no DOM access: `loadJob` normalizes and indexes the job, `validate`/`advise` report problems and capacity budgets, `place` computes every box's geometry, `route` turns connections into channel-routed polylines, and `render` emits the finished SVG string. The UI in `index.html` is a thin shell that re-runs the pipeline on every edit and injects the SVG. Because the engine is headless, the entire behavior is testable from `tests.html` without a browser UI, and print/export are just the same render call with different options.

For the full story — data model, router design, catalog discipline, and how to rebuild the whole thing from scratch — see **[BUILDING.md](BUILDING.md)**. The original frozen spec is in the project's `DESIGN.md` (kept in the dev workspace).
